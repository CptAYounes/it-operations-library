# Shift handover checklist

Complete this before responsibility changes. A ticket list without current impact, evidence and a next action is not a usable handover.

## Establish current state

- [ ] Summarise overall service health and any degraded or uncertain areas.
- [ ] Reconcile active alerts with open incidents and known maintenance.
- [ ] Identify urgent, high-impact or time-bound work first.
- [ ] Note security, safety, data-integrity or access concerns separately.

## Open issues

For every item being handed over:

- [ ] State the affected system/service and user impact.
- [ ] Give the current observed state, not only the original report.
- [ ] Link the incident, change or troubleshooting record.
- [ ] Summarise checks and actions already completed without hiding failed attempts.
- [ ] Identify the working hypothesis and its confidence.
- [ ] Provide the next safe action, owner and due time.
- [ ] State stop conditions and the escalation path.
- [ ] Confirm evidence is stored where the receiving person can access it.

## Changes and planned work

- [ ] List changes completed during the shift and their validation state.
- [ ] Identify monitoring or automation that was paused, silenced or overridden.
- [ ] List approved work due in the next shift, its impact and readiness state.
- [ ] Call out overlapping work, expiring windows and rollback constraints.

## Transfer responsibility

- [ ] Use a verbal or live handover for critical items when policy requires it.
- [ ] Give the receiver time to question ambiguous points.
- [ ] Confirm the receiver has required access and contact/escalation routes.
- [ ] Record who accepted the handover and when, including timezone.
- [ ] Update the shared record if state changes during the handover.

Do not copy credentials, private contact details or confidential customer data into a general handover note.
